# NASA-TLX administration and scoring: primary-source check

Scope: the original **weighted NASA Task Load Index (NASA-TLX)** and the later **Raw TLX (RTLX)** variant. This note is an implementation reference, not a substitute for recording the exact protocol used in a test. Source checked 2026-10-15.

## Source identity and rights

- Sandra G. Hart, *NASA Task Load Index (TLX): Paper and Pencil Package*, version 1.0 (NASA Ames Research Center, 1986), [NASA-hosted manual](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/nasa-tlx-v1-0-searchable-text-and-forms.pdf), [NTRS record 20000021488](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20000021488). The NTRS record labels it public and “Work of the US Gov. Public Use Permitted.” Manual printed pages and appendices below are the locators, not PDF viewer page numbers.
- Sandra G. Hart, “NASA-Task Load Index (NASA-TLX); 20 Years Later,” *Proceedings of the Human Factors and Ergonomics Society Annual Meeting* 50 (2006), 904–908, [NASA-hosted author paper](https://humansystems.arc.nasa.gov/groups/TLX/downloads/HFES_2006_Paper.pdf), section discussing modifications/Raw TLX. Hart characterizes RTLX as an unweighted variant whose six ratings are averaged or added, and reports that comparative sensitivity findings were mixed. Do not present RTLX as the original weighted protocol or a universally equivalent substitute.

It is not a blanket determination about all third-party content or NASA insignia/endorsement.

## Six rating dimensions

Use all six dimensions for either variant. NASA’s [manual, Appendix A, printed p. 13](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/nasa-tlx-v1-0-searchable-text-and-forms.pdf#page=15) defines their meaning; [Appendix C, printed p. 17](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/nasa-tlx-v1-0-searchable-text-and-forms.pdf#page=19) shows the rating sheet.

| Dimension | What the participant rates (paraphrase of Appendix A) | Low-end → high-end anchor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mental Demand | Amount of mental/perceptual activity needed | Low → High |
| Physical Demand | Amount of physical activity needed | Low → High |
| Temporal Demand | Time pressure from the pace of task events | Low → High |
| Performance | Perceived success in meeting the task goals | **Good → Poor** |
| Effort | Mental and physical work needed to achieve that performance | Low → High |
| Frustration | Insecurity, discouragement, irritation, stress and annoyance versus contentment/relaxation | Low → High |

**Direction check:** Performance is intentionally reversed relative to colloquial “higher performance is better”: the original rating sheet places *Good* at the left/low end and *Poor* at the right/high end. Higher numeric Performance ratings therefore mean **worse perceived performance** in the workload composite. Do not reverse-code it a second time. [Manual §6, printed p. 11, and Appendix C, printed p. 17](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/nasa-tlx-v1-0-searchable-text-and-forms.pdf#page=13).

## Administration — original weighted TLX

1. **Specify the task and rating unit.** Identify the participant, task/condition and the task or segment that each rating will cover. NASA permits ratings during a task, after segments, or after the entire task; select and document the timing consistently. [Manual §§2.3, 3.3, printed pp. 4–5](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/nasa-tlx-v1-0-searchable-text-and-forms.pdf#page=6).
2. **Explain all six scales and practice.** Give participants the scale definitions and instructions, resolve questions, and let them practice after a few tasks so that they develop a stable use of the scales. The NASA manual supplies participant-facing language in §6; this summary paraphrases the procedure instead of reproducing the full script. [Manual §§3.1–3.2 and 6, printed pp. 4, 11](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/nasa-tlx-v1-0-searchable-text-and-forms.pdf#page=6).
3. **Collect six magnitude ratings for every task condition of interest.** After each specified task/segment, have the participant mark each of the six anchored scales according to their experience of *that* task. Use the original 0–100 scale in five-point increments: its 21 tick marks form 20 equal intervals. If a mark falls between two ticks, the manual takes the tick to its right (round upward). Preserve individual dimension ratings, not just the composite. [Manual §§2.3, 3.3 and Appendix C, printed pp. 4–5, 17](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/nasa-tlx-v1-0-searchable-text-and-forms.pdf#page=6).
4. **Obtain 15 source-of-workload choices for each distinct task structure.** Present all unordered pairs of the six dimension titles (6 choose 2 = 15), one pair at a time, in a different randomized order for each participant. For each pair, ask which member contributed more to workload in the relevant task. Do this after the participant has experienced at least some relevant task conditions. The manual’s usual sequence places these choices after task ratings, but explicitly says the exact time of weighting is not critical once the participant has had that exposure. A participant needs a new set of weights for a distinctly different task; the same weights may be reused across difficulty variants of a structurally similar task when the sources of workload are comparable. [Manual §§2.2, 3.4 and 7; Appendix B, printed pp. 3, 5, 12, 14–16](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/nasa-tlx-v1-0-searchable-text-and-forms.pdf#page=5).
5. **Score within each participant and task condition.** Count how often each dimension was selected across the 15 pairs: its weight is an integer 0–5 and all six weights must sum to 15. Multiply each dimension's 0–100 rating by its own weight, sum the six products, and divide by 15: `weighted TLX = Σ(rating_i × weight_i) / 15`. Keep the six ratings and six weights alongside the composite. The manual's tally sheet explicitly checks total weight = 15 and no individual weight > 5. [Manual §§2.4, 4; Appendices D–E, printed pp. 4, 6, 18–19](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/nasa-tlx-v1-0-searchable-text-and-forms.pdf#page=7).

The original manual's procedure is *not* “get weights before the study”: its ordinary sequence is instructions → practice → task ratings → pairwise weights, while allowing weights after sufficient task exposure at another point. [Manual §§3.1–3.4, printed pp. 4–5](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/nasa-tlx-v1-0-searchable-text-and-forms.pdf#page=6). Hart’s later 2006 overview describes an alternative timing convention; that does not erase the manual's explicit exposure requirement. Record the actual timing used.

## Administration — Raw TLX variant

Give the same six dimension definitions and anchored ratings for each pre-specified task/segment, with the same Performance direction and timing/recording discipline. **Do not administer the 15 pairwise comparisons or calculate participant-specific weights** if the intended score is RTLX. Set the composite to the arithmetic mean of the six ratings on the 0–100 metric: `RTLX mean = Σ(rating_i) / 6`. Hart notes that some authors sum rather than average; if a sum is used, name it as a six-rating sum (0–600 on this response metric) and do not compare its numeric level directly with 0–100 weighted or mean RTLX. [Hart 2006, discussion of modifications/Raw TLX](https://humansystems.arc.nasa.gov/groups/TLX/downloads/HFES_2006_Paper.pdf); [NASA *Human Integration Design Handbook*, Revision 1, §5.7.3.4.1.3.2, printed p. 218](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/human-integration-design-handbook-revision-1.pdf).

## Implementation cautions

- Label the variant and score formula explicitly in reports and UI. “NASA-TLX” alone can hide whether pairwise weighting was performed. [Hart 2006, modifications/Raw TLX discussion](https://humansystems.arc.nasa.gov/groups/TLX/downloads/HFES_2006_Paper.pdf).
- Use task/condition-specific ratings and participant-specific weights; never average weights across people before computing their individual composites. This follows directly from the manual's per-participant worksheet and score procedure (§4), rather than being a separate NASA rule.
- Do not remove or rewrite a subscale while still claiming unchanged NASA-TLX validity. Hart 2006 warns that subscale additions, deletions or redefinitions yield a modified instrument requiring its own validity, sensitivity and reliability work. [Hart 2006, discussion of modifications](https://humansystems.arc.nasa.gov/groups/TLX/downloads/HFES_2006_Paper.pdf).
- The NASA official webpage labels itself historical and not actively maintained. Its permission statement was checked on 2026-10-15, but these citations do not establish NASA endorsement of this repository. [NASA TLX webpage](https://www.nasa.gov/human-systems-integration-division/nasa-task-load-index-tlx/).
