# Elicitation field guide: interviewing and observing pilots/maintainers

**Purpose:** derive operational goals, expert cognition, information needs, controls and performance criteria without allowing the present UI to define the answer.

## Pre-session briefing
- Define study objective, operator population, scenario family, system maturity, known risks and approval/security boundaries.
- Identify roles: lead interviewer, optional second analyst, operator SME, subject-matter adjudicator.
- Obtain appropriate consent/authorization for recordings. Do not record/export restricted workplace discussions to personal devices.
- Prepare an initial scenario narrative and known system-function map; explicitly distinguish document-based assumptions from observed realities.
- Avoid demonstrating proposed interface design until the independent needs elicitation is complete.

## Part A — task story (10–20 min)
1. What operational outcome were you responsible for?
2. Where in the mission/maintenance sequence did this begin/end?
3. What event cued the work? What told you the task was complete?
4. What could change the plan? How did you detect exceptions?
5. Which other operators, units or systems affected your choices?
6. What tasks competed for your attention or hands?
7. What did you do when information was missing/contradictory?
8. What does an inexperienced person tend to miss?

## Part B — goal-directed decision and information prompts
For **each decision**, record:
- What question are you answering? What alternatives are possible?
- What makes the decision necessary *now*, rather than later?
- What cues indicate that choice A vs B is appropriate? Which cues conflict?
- What must be known, understood or predicted before acting?
- How accurate/recent/credible must that evidence be?
- What information do you actually have now? What is missing or misleading?
- What happens if information arrives late or is incorrect?
- Which conditions make the usual cue unreliable?
- If no display existed, how would you know or infer the required state?

## Part C — ACTA knowledge audit (select applicable probes)
Past and future: What must be anticipated? What unexpected signs matter?
Big picture: How do you tell whether the situation is developing normally?
Noticing: Which subtle cues do experienced operators catch?
Job smarts: What workarounds and mental shortcuts are used, and why?
Anomalies: What is a meaningful surprise rather than background variation?
Misconceptions: What novice interpretation leads to the wrong action?
Self-monitoring: How do experts know their judgment may be wrong?
Difficult cases: Where does standard procedure stop being enough?
Represent responses as **cue → interpretation → decision → action → outcome**, with context and source.

## Part D — critical-incident/CDM sweeps
Only apply to a documented remembered event. Sweep 1: uninterrupted narrative. Sweep 2: chronological events with state/timing. Sweep 3: decision-point probes: noticed cues, interpretations, expectations, option set, uncertainty, goal conflicts, time pressure, novice differences. Sweep 4: counterfactuals: altered cue, absent cue, reversed option, degraded automation, cross-team effects. Produce the timeline and separate verified facts from retrospective reconstructions. Do NOT present counterfactual responses as observed behavior.

## Part E — control needs and feedback
- What state/function must you be able to influence? What is your intended result?
- Under which preconditions is intervention possible/authorized/forbidden?
- What parameter, range, rate, precision and temporal urgency characterize the input?
- How do you know the command was entered, accepted, begun, completed and actually achieved?
- What can inhibit/delay/override/interrupt it? How do you find out?
- Can you reverse/recover from an unintended action?
- How does control availability change in automation or degraded modes?
- Does actuation conflict with flight control, gaze, PPE, reach or other concurrent demands?

## Part F — direct observation / simulator notes
Keep separate columns: time; observable events; statements; analyst inference; system state; independent source. Use timestamps for parallel activities. Mark cues that likely would not appear in retrospective reports. Include environmental and physiological constraints as recorded conditions, not assumed explanations.

## Anti-leading rules
- Ask about the goal before asking where a feature should be placed.
- Replace 'Would a red alert help?' with 'What change must be noticed, why, and by when?'
- Do not turn a preference into a requirement without tracing its operational consequence.
- Require negative cases: what cue could mislead; what command should *not* be available; what data could safely be omitted?
- Ask operator to walk through at least one example with inconsistent or unavailable information.

## Session artifacts
01 scenario log; 02 task/function map; 03 decision table; 04 cues and cognitive demands; 05 information needs; 06 control/feedback needs; 07 unknowns/conflicts; 08 provenance log; 09 SME-confirmation notes. Distinguish draft analyst interpretation from confirmed user statements.

## Coverage and sampling cautions
Use purposive sampling across experience levels and roles; avoid calling one expert a statistically representative sample. Pilot versus maintainer, nominal versus emergency and current versus future operations demand different sampling designs. Triangulate documents, observations, simulations and SME accounts when practical.

## Post-session
Produce structured findings within 24–48 hours when feasible. Review ambiguities with the participant; adjudicate technical facts with engineering sources; keep dissent/alternatives where justified. Do not fabricate timestamps or turn inferred performance targets into validated thresholds.
